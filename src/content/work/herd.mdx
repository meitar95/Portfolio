why: >-
  Afimilk's herd system runs 15,000+ dairy farms in 50+ countries and tracks over 10
  million cows. Milk yield, fat and protein, conductivity, all the signals that catch
  things like mastitis early. The people using it are farm managers and farmers, 30 to
  70 years old, who spend most of the day outside and come into the office to log data
  and check on the herd. If they can't find what they need, all that data just sits there.
role: Lead Designer via UI Human Factors
team: Head of user research, UX specialist, UI art director, project manager, veterinarian consultant, engineers
timeline: ~1 year as lead designer
platform: Agri-tech, B2B web

outcomes:
  - value: "15K+"
    label: dairy farms in 50+ countries on the redesigned system
  - value: "Beta"
    label: testing with real users showed onboarding got easier
  - value: "4"
    label: core areas rebuilt on one shared set of components

solution:
  summary: >-
    We focused every screen on the job farmers came to do. Less decoration, more data,
    and the features that only made sense in the office got cleaned out.
  points:
    - title: Data first
      text: Decoration out, numbers in. The tables hold more columns now and still feel calmer.
    - title: Progress at a glance
      text: Tasks are grouped by type, and each one shows what's done today and what's left.
    - title: Based on real use
      text: Feedback showed us what farmers don't use, so office-only features came out.
    - title: One look everywhere
      text: Dashboard, herd view, tasks and settings all share the same components and colors.

learned: >-
  My biggest takeaway was to ask people what they don't use. Afimilk is really close to
  its farmers, and that showed us which features only made sense in the office. Cutting
  them helped new users more than anything we added. Next time I'd ask sharper
  questions too. There was one small data point I simplified instead of digging into,
  and I still wonder what was behind it.

## The problem

The system had tried to be trendy and elegant, and got buried under graphic add-ons that didn't age well. New users struggled to find key features, and the design system couldn't handle the amount of data on screen.
