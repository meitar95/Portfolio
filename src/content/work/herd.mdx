why: >-
  Afimilk's herd system runs 15,000+ dairy farms in 50+ countries and tracks more
  than 10 million cows: milk yield, fat and protein, conductivity, the signals that
  catch mastitis or ketosis early. Its users are farm directors and farmers aged 30
  to 70 who spend most of the day outdoors and come to the office to log data and
  check on the herd. If they can't find what they need, the data never turns into action.
role: Detail designer, promoted to lead designer during the project
team: Head of user research, UX specialist, UI art director, project manager, veterinarian consultant, engineers
timeline: ~3 years · the last as lead designer

outcomes:
  - value: "15K+"
    label: dairy farms in 50+ countries on the redesigned system
  - value: "Beta"
    label: testing with real users confirmed onboarding got easier
  - value: "Lead"
    label: promoted to lead designer, then led the field app redesign

solution:
  summary: >-
    Every screen focused on the job. Minimal, organized, data first. Features built
    for the office that farmers didn't use in the field were cleaned out.
  points:
    - title: Data first
      text: Decoration out, numbers in. Tables carry more columns and still read calmer.
    - title: Progress, not counts
      text: Tasks grouped by type, each showing what's done and what's left today.
    - title: Built from use, not assumptions
      text: Feedback showed what farmers don't use. Office-only features were removed.
    - title: One language
      text: Dashboard, herd view, tasks and settings share one set of components and colors.

learned: >-
  Ask what people don't use, not only what they want. Afimilk's close contact with
  farmers showed us which features only made sense in the office, and cutting them
  did more for new users than anything we added.

## The problem

The system had tried to look trendy and elegant, and got lost in graphic add-ons that didn't age well. New users struggled to find key features, and the design system couldn't carry the amount of data on screen.

### Two details, zoomed in
