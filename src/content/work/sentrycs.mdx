why: >-
  Sentrycs finds unauthorized drones and takes control of them, at airports, military
  bases, prisons and ports. Operators were happy with how it worked. The problem was how
  it looked: old and generic, for a young company showing off serious tech at
  conferences. And on field tablets the main text was 11–12px, in a system where
  missing an alert really matters.
role: Product Designer. UI designer under a senior lead, then sole designer for the final year.
team: Design lead (year one), product, engineering
timeline: ~2 years

outcomes:
  - value: "1 → 8"
    label: one concept screen grown into an 8-module system
  - value: "16px"
    label: body text, up from 11–12px, tested with ~20 users
  - value: "~90%"
    label: design-to-code fidelity, against an 80% target

solution:
  summary: >-
    Everything operators already knew stayed where it was. Everything they look at got
    redone, from the live map to analytics, and it all lives in a style guide the dev
    team actually used.
  points:
    - title: Same workflow
      text: Same layout, navigation, logic and microcopy. Nobody had to relearn a live defense tool.
    - title: Text you can read
      text: 16px body text with a 14px minimum on a clean rem scale. I tested it with about 20 users on tablet and desktop.
    - title: A map language
      text: I redrew drone states, markers and zones to match what's common in the market, then tested them on real terrain.
    - title: Glass that ships
      text: The glass effect looked great in Figma and was tricky in code. I worked it out with the developers and ran design QA until we hit ~90%.

learned: >-
  This one taught me you can give a product a whole new look without making anyone
  relearn it. The workflow stayed and the face changed. Seeing the new design front
  and center in Sentrycs' own marketing videos still makes me smile.

## The problem

There were two problems, and both mattered. The product didn't look like the technology behind it, and the text operators rely on in the field was too small to read safely.

<ReadabilityScale />

## The evidence

The lead designer made the first concept screen. My part was figuring out how that one screen turns into a whole product, and later, as the only designer, getting it shipped.

<Principles />

### One screen, broken into parts

Every module uses the same building blocks. Hover or tap one to see where it sits.

<ScreenAnatomy />

### A style guide made for this product

Generic UI kits don't come with drone states, no-fly zones or satellite maps, so the style guide had to cover them.

<StyleGuide />
