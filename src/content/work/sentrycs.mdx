  Sentrycs detects and takes control of unauthorized drones at airports, military
  bases, prisons and ports. Operators were happy with how the system worked, but the
  interface looked old and generic, a problem for a young company showing
  cutting-edge security at conferences. And in the field, core text ran at 11–12px
  on tablets, in a system where a missed alert has real consequences.
role: Product Designer. UI designer under a senior lead, then sole designer for the final year.
team: Design lead (year one), product, engineering

  - value: "1 → 8"
    label: one concept screen scaled into an 8-module system
  - value: "16px"
    label: body text, up from 11–12px, tested with ~20 users
  - value: "~90%"
    label: design-to-code fidelity, against an 80% target

solution:
  summary: >-
    Keep everything operators already know; upgrade everything they see. One visual
    language, from the live map to analytics, built as a style guide the dev team
    adopted.
  points:
    - title: UX preserved
      text: Same layout, navigation, operating principles and microcopy, so nobody had to relearn a live defense tool.
    - title: A readability system
      text: 16px body and a 14px floor on a clean rem scale, validated with ~20 users on tablet and desktop.
    - title: A map language
      text: Drone states, markers and zones redrawn to market conventions and tested on real terrain.
    - title: Glass that ships
      text: Solved the Figma glass effect in code with engineering, and owned Design QA to ~90% fidelity.

learned: >-
  A redesign doesn't have to make people relearn. By protecting the UX operators
  trusted and changing only what they see, we gave a cutting-edge product a face that
  matched it, and it now leads Sentrycs' own marketing videos.

## The problem

Two problems, both real. The product's look didn't match the technology behind it, and the text operators depend on in the field was too small to read safely.
