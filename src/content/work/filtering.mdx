---
title: From four filter bars to one
tagline: Every meeting opened with how much people used filtering. Then I watched the session recordings.
card: Everyone saw filtering as our most-used feature. The recordings showed power users fighting it. I turned four filter patterns into one, with an AI helper built in.
order: 1
visual: filtering-before-after

why: >-
  Filtering was the most-used feature in the product, and we were proud of that number.
  In the recordings, power users at 3 clients clicked the same filters over and over,
  then left mid-search, a few times a day. They weren't going to complain. They'd just slowly stop using the product, and that could
  have cost us more than one client.
role: Sole Product Designer
team: Product lead, PM, engineering, customer success
timeline: 1 month · fast track · 2026
platform: WAF & API security, B2B SaaS
confidential: true

outcomes:
  - value: "4 → 1"
    label: filtering patterns, now one system across 6 modules
  - value: "3"
    label: at-risk clients I spotted in the recordings
  - value: "~10"
    label: users tested the flow before engineering started

solution:
  summary: >-
    Filtering is basic stuff, and nobody should have to learn a new way to do it. So I
    went with the classic model people already know from other tools, and made it work
    the same way, with the same words, in every module.
  points:
    - title: Ask AI
      text: Product asked for an AI helper. I decided where it lives and how it behaves, right in the filter bar, so asking in plain words changes the table itself.
    - title: Simple / Advanced
      text: The basics up front. The classic field, operator and value builder is one click away for power users.
    - title: Grouped filters
      text: 28 flat fields sorted into families, and each customer only sees the ones that apply to them.
    - title: Save View
      text: Build a filter set once and come back to it tomorrow, so daily investigations don't start from zero.

learned: >-
  I used to read high usage as good news. This project changed that for me. Our
  most-used feature was where people struggled the most, and they blamed themselves
  instead of telling us. These days I watch a few recordings before I trust a dashboard.

## The problem

While designing a new Findings page, I noticed the filters acting weird and asked the dev team to take a look. Meanwhile I went into Amplitude to see how our power users were really doing with the tool they use most.

<VoiceOfCustomer />

Under all of that was a structure problem. The product grew one module at a time, and every module got its own way to filter.

## The evidence

From there I kept coming back to one question. What do people already expect a filter to do? Most of the answers were out there, in tools they use every day. My job was to pick the ones that felt most natural.

## The result

Early client feedback was really good, and the new filtering helped close new deals. It's rolling out to everyone now, and we're watching the same session recordings to see the difference. It was also our first real step toward making all six modules feel like one product.
