why: >-
  Cybersixgill collected threat intelligence from the deep and dark web: forums,
  markets, paste sites, chat apps. Analysts came to it with one question: what
  threatens my organization right now? The same screens had to answer it for a
  five-person security team and for an enterprise with thousands of alerts and
  more than eleven thousand assets to watch.
role: Product Designer, through UI – Human Factors
team: Design team at UI – Human Factors, Cybersixgill product and engineering
timeline: ~1 year · 2022
platform: Threat intelligence, B2B web

outcomes:
  - value: "10 → 10K"
    label: every widget designed to hold numbers from ten to ten thousand
  - value: "1 grid"
    label: for every dashboard widget, so new ones drop in without a redesign
  - value: "11K+"
    label: assets the inventory was designed to manage, ranked by risk

solution:
  summary: >-
    A dashboard built from widgets on one strict grid, every widget drawn at three
    data volumes, and an asset inventory that lets teams decide once what to monitor.
  points:
    - title: One widget grid
      text: 3 columns by 3 rows make a small widget, 16px between them, aligned to the search bar.
    - title: Built for volume
      text: Each widget drawn at regular, 100+ and 1k+ data, so big numbers never break a layout.
    - title: Inventory, ranked by risk
      text: Domains, IPs, executives and more in one table, with what's new and what's at risk on top.
    - title: Policies, not chores
      text: Teams set what to watch and what to exclude once, instead of cleaning the list by hand.

learned: >-
  Security dashboards are judged at their worst moment, the day the numbers spike.
  Designing every widget for the biggest customer first, then checking it still
  felt calm for the smallest, kept one system working for both.

## The problem

A threat feed is only useful if it fits on screen. The number in a widget could be 10 for one customer and 10,000 for the next, and the layout had to survive both.

<NumberScale />

## The evidence

### One grid for every widget

Instead of designing dashboards screen by screen, we set one rule every widget follows. New widgets drop into the grid without a redesign.

<WidgetGrid />

### An inventory that cleans itself

Attack surface monitoring added thousands of assets per customer. The flow we designed keeps that list useful without someone pruning it by hand.
