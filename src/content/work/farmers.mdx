solution:
  summary: >-
    Less app, not more. The home screen became today's tasks, every task became one
    short flow, and the wand farmers already carry became the fastest way in.
  points:
    - title: Today's work first
      text: Home is a list of today's tasks with progress per group, not a dashboard of counters.
    - title: One flow per task
      text: Find the cow, do the thing, move to the next. Same flow from a task or from the cow card.
    - title: Built for the wand
      text: Type an ID or tap a tag with the wand. Bulk jobs like tagging stay open for the next cow.
    - title: Nothing that only looks tappable
      text: If it looks like a button it is one. Cards that only looked tappable and tooltips that wouldn't close are gone.

learned: >-
  Low adoption isn't always a skills problem. Farmers handle complex work all day;
  what they couldn't spare was extra thinking between one cow and the next. We
  stopped adding features and took away everything that stood between a farmer and
  the task, and that's when the app replaced the binder.

## The problem

The app could do a lot. Farmers opened it, found a cow, and left without logging anything. The task that makes the whole system work was the one nobody finished.

<DataFindings />

## The evidence

The numbers told us what was happening, not why. So we went to the farms: visits, about 15 contextual interviews, and remote sessions where farmers shared their screen and used the app while we watched.

<FieldNotes />

### Not tech literacy, cognitive load

Farmers manage complex herds and equipment every day. The problem wasn't skill. Between one cow and the next, the app asked them to think: which card opens, where the list ends, how to close a tooltip. Paper never asked them anything.

### One flow, rebuilt around the barn

Tag assignment is a good example. The data showed farms tag many cows in a row, long after setup, so the flow is built for a line of cows and a wand in hand.

<TagFlow />

We also looked at swipe and multi-select patterns from other apps. Long swipe gestures were ruled out early as a poor fit for our users.

**Result:** the redesign shipped and held up in field testing in New Zealand and Italy with only minor bugs. Logging moved from paper into the app:
