## The problem

The app could do a lot. Farmers opened it, found a cow, and left without logging anything. The one task that makes the whole system work was the one nobody finished.

## The evidence

The numbers showed us what was happening but not why, so we went to the farms. We visited, ran about 15 contextual interviews, and joined remote sessions where farmers shared their screen and used the app while we watched.

<FieldNotes />

### Too much thinking between cows

These farmers run complex herds and equipment every day, so skill wasn't the issue. The app kept making them think. Which card opens? Where does the list end? How do I close this tooltip? In UX terms the cognitive load was way too high for a job you do standing next to a cow. Paper never asked them anything.

### One flow, built around the barn

Tag assignment is a good example. The data showed farms tag lots of cows in a row, long after setup, so I built the flow for a line of cows and a wand in hand.

<TagFlow />

We also looked at swipe and multi-select patterns from other apps. Long swipes didn't fit how our users work, so we dropped them early.

## The result
